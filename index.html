Act I · The Scale of It

The Big Picture

One in four Australians
drinks above safe limits

Australia has one of the world's most embedded drinking cultures. Nearly 70% of adults drank in the past year, and more than a quarter regularly exceeded the national health guidelines. Below, how Australia divides up by drinking habit.

Act II · Where Australia Drinks

Geography of Drinking

The NT leads, Victoria trails —
but all states are drinking less

Risky alcohol consumption varies dramatically by state. The Northern Territory consistently records the highest rates, while Victoria is the most moderate. But every single state has reduced risky drinking since 2007.

Act III · What We Drink

The Changing Glass

Wine overtook beer in 2014.
Spirits are surging. RTDs collapsed.

The mix of what Australians drink has transformed dramatically. For most of history, beer dominated. But since 2014–15, wine has been the biggest contributor to total alcohol consumed. And since 2016, spirits have surged — driven by craft gin, premium whisky, and RTD cocktails — before a sharp 2023–24 reversal.

Act IV · Who Drinks

Demographics of Drinking

Young Australians binge.
Older Australians drink steadily — and too much.

The pattern of risky drinking splits sharply by age. Young adults aged 18–24 are most likely to exceed guidelines through single-occasion binge drinking. But Australians aged 55–64 have the highest rates of chronic weekly over-consumption — a quiet, under-reported risk.

Act V · The Cost

Human & Economic Cost

$72.9 billion a year.
Alcohol is Australia's most expensive habit.

Beyond health statistics, alcohol use carries a staggering economic burden. The estimated social cost in 2020–21 was $72.9 billion — projected to reach $75 billion by 2022–23. The biggest costs are not bars or hospitals — they are the lives cut short, and the years lost to illness.

Who's seeking treatment? Drug of concern, 2014–2024

Alcohol has consistently been the #1 reason Australians seek drug treatment — accounting for 42% of all episodes in 2023–24. Amphetamines are rising fast.

Source: AIHW Alcohol and Other Drug Treatment Services in Australia, 2023–24

Key insight: Almost 3 in 5 drug-related hospitalisations in 2023–24 involved alcohol — more than cannabis, methamphetamine and heroin combined. Alcohol is, by far, Australia's most harmful legal substance.

Act VI · The Long View

Trends Over Time

Two decades of change —
Australia is slowly sobering up

Risky drinking has declined across all states since 2007. Per capita consumption hit its modern peak in 2007–08 and has trended downward since. The 2023–24 data shows the steepest single-year drop on record. Younger Australians in particular are drinking less — driven by cost of living, health consciousness, and cultural shift.
